## 1️⃣ **Data Professional Survey Dashboard**
**Goal.**
The objective was to transform survey data into an interactive report that allows users to explore industry trends and compare different professional groups.

**Technologies used**
- Microsoft Power BI
- Power Query
- DAX
- Data visualisation
- Data cleaning and transformation

**Data Cleaning**
- Using *Power Query* to remove nulls and update field, such as salary, into suitable formats for visualisation.
- Standardising columns, such as Job Role, as in the survey free text was an option.
- Creating new measures and new columns, using *Bins* and *Groups*, to create ranges for columns such as Age for better visualisation and anlaysis.

**Key Features**
- Three-page interactive dashboard.
- Dynamic slicers with multiple-selection functionality.
- Clear-all-filters functionality.
- KPI cards and interactive visualisations.
- Respondent counts alongside salary comparisons.

**Design Notes**
- Main Page (1920x1080 canvas), secondary pages (1280x720 canvas)
- Consistent Theme and Layout across pages
- Conditional formatting on gauges to help showcase ratings.

**Key Findings**
 - Data Scientist is the top paid job role in the data sector outperforming the next highest average paying job role by nearly $30,000.
 - Specialist job roles tend to earn a higher salary than general/non technical job roles.
 - Professionals who perceived it easier to enter into the data sector earned more on average than those who found it more difficult. This trend is seen similarly between professionals who switched from another profession into data and those who are non-switchers.

- Those on a higher salary valued other things more in a job than their salary, such as the culture of the company they work for.
- For the younger generation the increase in education level did not have as big an impact on salary as it did on the older generation. This could be investigated further to find out if this is due to higher level of education becoming less important to the employers and companies, or if the higher level of education becomes more prevalent over time.
- The difference in salary between non-switcher and those who came from a different profession is negligible as both earn roughly the same on average.

- The main coding language of data professionals is python.
- Data analyst profession has the widest array of programming languages while the other industries are more language specific.
- The Finance and Tech industries are the top paying for data professionals and in most industries having relevant skills was more important than level of education in terms of level of salary.
